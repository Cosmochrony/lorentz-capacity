  \begin{abstract}
    The O-series saturation parameter $\eta_n^{\mathrm{O7}}$ is defined in~\cite{Beau2026a11}
    as the ratio of cumulative occupied projective capacity to total residual frontier novelty.
    The analytic factorization proved below explains why this total
    ratio satisfies a linear filling law $B_n + \Sigma_n^{\mathrm{tot}} = 1$ and does not
    by itself yield the Lorentz factor:
    $\omega_q(a,b,c) = e^{2\pi ic/q}\omega_q(a,b,0)$, so the central
    coordinate $c$ contributes only a global phase and is invisible to the fingerprint span.
    We identify the conditional mechanism by which a temporally projected residual
    $\Sigma_n^\tau := \mathcal{P}_\tau^{\mathrm{Q5b}}\,\Sigma_n^{\mathrm{tot}}$
    can produce the relativistic mobility ratio: under the
    Born--Infeld admissibility budget established in~\cite{Beau2026c}, a quadratic
    normalization of $B_n$ and $\Sigma_n^\tau$ implies
    \[
      \eta_n^\tau := \frac{B_n}{\Sigma_n^\tau} \longrightarrow \beta\gamma,
    \]
    where $\beta\gamma$ is the relativistic mobility and $\gamma$ is the Lorentz factor.
    Consequently, $\Phi(\eta_n^\tau) = 1/\sqrt{1+(\eta_n^\tau)^2}$ converges to $1/\gamma$.
    The temporal projection operator $\mathcal{P}_\tau^{\mathrm{Q5b}}$ is induced by the
    Carnot--Carath\'eodory geometry and co-metric closure of~\cite{Beau2026q5b,Beau2026q11}.
    Constructing this operator and verifying $\mathcal{P}_\tau^{\mathrm{Q5b}}\Sigma_n^{\mathrm{tot}}
    \to \sqrt{1-B_n^2}$ is the remaining technical task.
  \end{abstract}

  \section{O7 occupation and residual novelty}
    \label{sec:o7}

    The O7 saturation parameter is defined in~\cite{Beau2026a11} by
    \[
      \eta_n^{\mathrm{O7}}(x)
      =
      \frac{B_n(x)}{\Sigma_n(x)},
      \qquad
      B_n(x) := |\psi_n(x)|^2
      =
      \frac{|S_n \cap C_x|}{|C_x|}.
    \]
    Here $C_x$ is the effective resolution cell associated with the observable state $x$,
    and $S_n$ is the BFS-reachable set up to cascade depth $n$.

    The observable $B_n(x)$ is cumulative.
    It measures the fraction of the cell $C_x$ already reached by the BFS cascade by
    depth $n$.
    In particular,
    \[
      0 \leq B_n(x) \leq 1,
      \qquad
      B_{n+1}(x) \geq B_n(x).
    \]
    It is therefore an occupation observable, not an infinitesimal mobility.

    By contrast, $\Sigma_n(x)$ measures the average effective novelty contributed by
    the BFS frontier at depth $n$: the mean fraction of genuinely new projective
    direction brought by admissible paths reaching $\partial S_n$ within $C_x$.
    It is a residual frontier observable, instantaneous and non-increasing in $n$.

    The ratio $\eta_n^{\mathrm{O7}}$ is therefore not a fitting parameter.
    It is an occupied-over-residual capacity ratio:
    \[
      \eta_n^{\mathrm{O7}}
      =
      \frac{\text{cumulative occupied capacity}}{\text{residual frontier novelty}}.
    \]

    The question addressed in this note is whether this O7 ratio converges, in the
    continuum geometric limit, to the relativistic mobility ratio $dI_{\mathrm{space}}/dI_\tau$.

  \section{Algebraic Born--Infeld target}
    \label{sec:bi}

    The cascade depth $n$ is not a time coordinate.
    It is the ordinal depth of an admissible projective history, counting the number of
    distinguishable projective transitions undergone along that history~\cite{Beau2026c}.

    Assume a bounded Born--Infeld mobility budget at each projective depth
    increment $dn$:
    \[
      dI_\tau^2 + dI_{\mathrm{space}}^2 = c_{\mathrm{BI}}^2\,dn^2.
    \]
    Define the normalized spectral mobility
    \[
      \beta := \frac{dI_{\mathrm{space}}}{c_{\mathrm{BI}}\,dn}.
    \]
    Then the residual temporal component is
    \[
      dI_\tau = c_{\mathrm{BI}}\sqrt{1-\beta^2}\,dn,
    \]
    and therefore
    \[
      \frac{dI_{\mathrm{space}}}{dI_\tau}
      =
      \frac{\beta}{\sqrt{1-\beta^2}}
      =
      \beta\gamma,
      \qquad
      \gamma := \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\beta^2}}.
    \]
    If a spectral saturation parameter $\eta$ satisfies $\eta = dI_{\mathrm{space}}/dI_\tau$, then
    \[
      \Phi(\eta) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\eta^2}} = \frac{1}{\gamma}.
    \]
    The inverse Lorentz factor is the residual-capacity profile of a quadratic
    Born--Infeld budget.
    This step is purely algebraic.
    The non-trivial question is whether the O7 total residual $\Sigma_n$ contains
    a temporal component $\Sigma_n^\tau$ that converges to the normalized temporal
    budget component $\sqrt{1-\beta^2}$.

  \section{Why the reduced filling model is insufficient}
    \label{sec:linear}

    The reduced filling model of~\cite{Beau2026a11} gives, before geometric correction,
    \[
      B_n \simeq \frac{n}{D},
      \qquad
      \Sigma_n \simeq 1 - \frac{n}{D},
    \]
    equivalently $B_n + \Sigma_n \simeq 1$.
    This is a linear residual law, describing monotone filling of a finite
    resolution cell.

    \paragraph{Numerical confirmation.}
      Direct computation on $\operatorname{Heis}_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$ for
      $q \in \{7, 11, 13\}$, using cumulative $(a,b)$ spatial coverage for $B_n$ and
      the total residual Weil-representation span for $\Sigma_n^{\mathrm{tot}}$,
      yields mean deviations:
      \[
        |B_n + \Sigma_n^{\mathrm{tot}} - 1| \approx 0.02\text{--}0.04,
        \qquad
        |B_n^2 + (\Sigma_n^{\mathrm{tot}})^2 - 1| \approx 0.17\text{--}0.26.
      \]
      The data confirm the linear law for the total residual.
      The quadratic law fails by a factor of approximately $1/\sqrt{1-B_n^2}$ relative
      to the linear law, indicating that $\Sigma_n^{\mathrm{tot}}$ is too small by this
      factor to be the Lorentzian residual.

    \paragraph{Interpretation.}
      The O7 total residual $\Sigma_n^{\mathrm{tot}}$ measures total Weil-space
      coverage and satisfies $B_n + \Sigma_n^{\mathrm{tot}} \simeq 1$.
      The Lorentzian residual factor $\sqrt{1-\beta^2}$ is not obtained from
      $\Sigma_n^{\mathrm{tot}}$ directly.
      It requires projecting $\Sigma_n^{\mathrm{tot}}$ onto the \emph{temporal
  component} selected by the Q5b principal-symbol geometry:
      \[
        \Sigma_n^\tau := \mathcal{P}_\tau^{\mathrm{Q5b}}\,\Sigma_n^{\mathrm{tot}},
      \]
      where $\mathcal{P}_\tau^{\mathrm{Q5b}}$ is the temporal residual projection
      induced by the completed effective co-metric.
      The corrected quadratic target is
      \[
        B_n^2 + (\Sigma_n^\tau)^2 \longrightarrow 1.
      \]

  \section{Weil factorization and the invisibility of the central direction}
  \label{sec:weil-factorization}

  \subsection{The Schr\"odinger representation and its factorization}

    The Schr\"odinger (Weil) representation of $\operatorname{Heis}_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$
    acts on $\ell^2(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{C}^q$ by
    \[
      [\omega_q(a,b,c)\,\psi](x)
      =
      e^{2\pi i(c+ax)/q}\,\psi(x+b \bmod q).
    \]

    \begin{proposition}[Central factorization]
      \label{prop:factorization}
      For all $(a,b,c) \in \operatorname{Heis}_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$,
      \[
        \omega_q(a,b,c) = e^{2\pi ic/q}\cdot\omega_q(a,b,0).
      \]
    \end{proposition}

    \begin{proof}
      Direct from the formula: $[\omega_q(a,b,c)\psi](x) = e^{2\pi ic/q}\cdot e^{2\pi iax/q}\psi(x+b)
      = e^{2\pi ic/q}\cdot [\omega_q(a,b,0)\psi](x)$.
    \end{proof}

  \subsection{Consequence: central direction is invisible to span}

    Proposition~\ref{prop:factorization} has an immediate corollary for the fingerprint span.

    \begin{corollary}[Linear filling law]
      \label{cor:linear}
      The span of $\{\omega_q(g) : g \in \bigcup_{k \leq n} S_k\}$ in
      $\operatorname{End}(\mathbb{C}^q)$ depends only on the projected $(a,b)$ coordinates:
      \[
        \operatorname{span}\{\omega_q(a,b,c)\}
        =
        \operatorname{span}\{\omega_q(a,b,0)\}.
      \]
      The span dimension at depth $n$ equals the number of distinct $(a,b)$ pairs in
      $\bigcup_{k \leq n} S_k$.
      On $\operatorname{Heis}_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$ with generators
      $\{(\pm1,0,0),(0,\pm1,0)\}$, the BFS explores the $L^1$ ball on
      $(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^2$:
      \[
        B_n
        =
        \frac{|\{(a,b) : \min(a,q-a)+\min(b,q-b) \leq n\}|}{q^2}
        =
        \frac{2n^2+2n+1}{q^2}
        \quad (n < q/2),
      \]
      and the total residual novelty satisfies:
      \[
        \Sigma_n^{\mathrm{tot}} = 1 - B_n.
      \]
    \end{corollary}

    The linear law $B_n + \Sigma_n^{\mathrm{tot}} = 1$ is therefore \emph{exact} in the
    Schr\"odinger representation, not an approximation.
    The central coordinate $c$ is invisible to the fingerprint span: it contributes only
    a global phase $e^{2\pi ic/q}$, which does not change the span direction.

  \subsection{Why this does not obstruct the Lorentz identification}

    The factorization shows that the temporal direction required by the Lorentz
    identification is \emph{not} directly visible in the Schr\"odinger representation.
    This is expected: in Carnot--Carath\'eodory geometry, the central direction $Z$
    appears at degree~2 via the bracket $[X,Y] = Z$, not at degree~1 alongside the
    horizontal generators.
    It becomes a metric temporal direction only after the symbol completion chain
    \[
      \text{CC structure}
      \;\to\;
      \text{sub-principal symbol correction}
      \;\to\;
      \text{Q11 co-metric closure}
      \;\to\;
      g^{\mu\nu} = 2\,\eta^{\mu\nu}.
    \]
    Consequently, the projection $\mathcal{P}_\tau^{\mathrm{Q5b}}$ of
    Definition~\ref{def:q5b-temporal-projection} does \emph{not} act on the
    Schr\"odinger representation space $\mathbb{C}^q$ directly.
    It acts on the \emph{capacity functional} $\Sigma_n^{\mathrm{tot}}$ viewed as a
    section of the admissible capacity bundle over the Q5b Carnot limit, projecting
    onto the component induced by the completed co-metric.

    The factorization result therefore clarifies the structure of the remaining proof:
    one must go beyond the Schr\"odinger representation and work at the level of the
    Q5b principal-symbol geometry to extract the temporal residual $\Sigma_n^\tau$.

  \section{Numerical tests for candidates of \texorpdfstring{$\Sigma_n^\tau$}{Sigma\_n\^{}tau}}
  \label{sec:numerical}

  \subsection{Setup and candidates}

    The Weil factorization of Section~\ref{sec:weil-factorization} identifies the
    central coordinate $c$ as invisible to the fingerprint span.
    The projection $\mathcal{P}_\tau^{\mathrm{Q5b}}$ must therefore extract the
    temporal residual from outside the Schr\"odinger representation.
    To probe what kind of observable could fill this role, we test three natural
    candidates on $\operatorname{Heis}_3(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})$ for
    $q \in \{11,13,17\}$.

    With $B_n = (2n^2+2n+1)/q^2$ (proved in Section~\ref{sec:weil-factorization}),
    the candidates are:

    \begin{itemize}
      \item $\Sigma_n^A$ : residual $c$-value coverage,
      $1 - |\{c : \exists(a,b),\,(a,b,c)\in\bigcup_{k\leq n}S_k\}|/q$.
      This is the fraction of central values not yet reached by the BFS.
      \item $\Sigma_n^B = \sqrt{1-B_n}$ :
      the square-root complement of spatial coverage.
      By construction it satisfies $B_n + (\Sigma_n^B)^2 = 1$, so it serves
      as a reference for the ``half-linear'' law.
      \item $\Sigma_n^C = \sqrt{1-\beta_n^2}$ with $\beta_n = n/n_{\max}$ :
      the velocity-proxy complement, where $\beta_n$ is the normalized BFS depth.
    \end{itemize}

    For each candidate $\Sigma_n^X$ we measure the quadratic sum
    $B_n^2 + (\Sigma_n^X)^2$ and compare to~$1$.

      The depth proxy is not the correct velocity coordinate.

  \subsection{Interpretation}

  No natural BFS observable satisfies the quadratic law $B_n^2+(\Sigma_n^\tau)^2
  \to 1$ with $B_n = (2n^2+2n+1)/q^2$.
  The failure modes are structurally distinct:
  $\Sigma_n^A$ saturates too fast (scaling mismatch);
  $\Sigma_n^B$ satisfies a linear-square law, not a fully quadratic one;
  $\Sigma_n^C$ is close but biased in the high-$\beta$ regime.

  This confirms that $\mathcal{P}_\tau^{\mathrm{Q5b}}$ cannot be replaced by
  any simple function of the BFS cascade observables.
  The temporal projection must be extracted from the capacity functional at the
  level of the Q5b completed co-metric, as stated in
  Definition~\ref{def:q5b-temporal-projection}.
  Constructing this projection explicitly from Q5b~\S3--4 is the remaining
  open task.

  \section{Consequence for the Lorentz factor}
  \label{sec:consequence}

  \begin{theorem}[Lorentz factor from O7 residual capacity, conditional on Lemma~\ref{lem:main}]
    \label{thm:lorentz}
    Assume the Born--Infeld mobility budget of Section~\ref{sec:bi} and the
    convergence of Lemma~\ref{lem:main}.
    Define the temporally projected saturation parameter
    \[
      \eta_n^\tau := \frac{B_n}{\Sigma_n^\tau}
      = \frac{B_n}{\mathcal{P}_\tau^{\mathrm{Q5b}}\,\Sigma_n^{\mathrm{tot}}}.
    \]
    Then
    \[
      \eta_n^\tau
      \longrightarrow
      \frac{\beta}{\sqrt{1-\beta^2}}
      =
      \beta\gamma,
    \]
    and therefore the O-series profile with temporal projection satisfies
    \[
      \Phi\!\left(\eta_n^\tau\right)
      =
      \frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\left(\eta_n^\tau\right)^2}}
      \longrightarrow
      \frac{1}{\gamma}.
    \]
  \end{theorem}

  The special-relativistic residual factor is recovered from the temporally
  projected O7 ratio $B_n/\Sigma_n^\tau$.
  The O7 total ratio $\eta_n^{\mathrm{O7}} = B_n/\Sigma_n^{\mathrm{tot}}$ satisfies
  the linear law and does not directly yield the Lorentz factor; the Q5b temporal
  projection is the essential intermediate step.

  \begin{remark}[Projected saturation pressure at $v \to c$]
For the temporally projected analogue $P_{n,\tau}^{\mathrm{sat}} := \gamma\,\eta_n^\tau$,
one obtains $P_{n,\tau}^{\mathrm{sat}} \to \beta\gamma^2$ under $\eta_n^\tau \to \beta\gamma$,
which diverges as $v \to c$.
This is consistent with the light cone reading $dI_\tau = 0 \Leftrightarrow v = c$:
the budget is fully consumed by spatial mobility and no temporal residual remains.
Note that the O7 total pressure $P_n^{\mathrm{sat}} = \gamma\,\eta_n^{\mathrm{O7}}$
satisfies the linear law and does not exhibit this divergence directly.
  \end{remark}

  \begin{remark}[Scope]
Theorem~\ref{thm:lorentz} directly yields time dilation
($d\tau/dn = 1/\gamma$) and the light cone ($dI_\tau = 0 \Leftrightarrow v = c$).
Length contraction requires the additional reconstruction of effective Lorentz
transformations between inertial observers.
The extension to general relativity is expected to proceed through the non-uniform regime: when
localised configurations modify the local spectral budget, the position-dependent
metric $g_{\mu\nu}(x)$ encodes gravitational time dilation as a comparison of
residual temporal capacities along distinct projected histories.
  \end{remark}
